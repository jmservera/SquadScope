## Key Takeaways

### Week 2026-W45 — [Week 45, 2026](/weekly/2026/w45/)
- Gap to watch: Agent observability is the largest absence: there is no visible new cluster for action logs, permission boundaries, rollback, or behavioral testing, despite both press concern and increasingly consequential mobile access. Provenance is also thin. Creative skills and reference galleries can generate or transform assets, yet licensing, source lineage, and output attribution are not a visible technical theme. The sample also underrepresents robotics and scientific tooling relative to industry coverage, and shows little new work on energy or cost measurement for autonomous workflows. Finally, local-first assistants are appearing, but interoperable identity and policy controls across models and devices are not. Those missing layers will determine whether portable skills become dependable infrastructure or merely convenient demos.
- Closing read: Watch whether Android agents develop explicit permission models and reproducible task evaluations rather than accumulating integrations alone. The creative-skill wave should separate durable tools from prompt packaging as users demand measurable fidelity, provenance, and repeatability. Reverse-engineering interfaces are likely to keep expanding, but trust will depend on showing how conclusions were derived. November's next phase should be judged by whether oversight begins catching up with reach.

### Week 2026-W46 — [Week 46, 2026](/weekly/2026/w46/)
- Gap to watch: Agent permissions remain the largest absence. The crawl shows tools that can run persistently and operate phones, but not a corresponding wave of policy engines, tamper-evident action logs, revocation controls, or standardized evaluations. That gap matters more as agents leave the coding sandbox. Energy-aware inference and memory efficiency are also missing despite heavy press attention to data-center constraints and full-stack codesign. Accessibility is another disconnect: GitHub elevated repository-level accessibility statements, yet no visible new-project cluster tackles accessible agent interfaces or adaptive creative tooling. Finally, industrial domains highlighted in the press—6G, semiconductor fabs, public infrastructure, and robotics—have little representation beyond early simulation-oriented tools.
- Closing read: Watch whether background agents acquire explicit approval policies and audit trails, rather than only more integrations. Mobile control is likely to keep expanding, but projects that make interruption and recovery first-class will be more consequential than another device wrapper. Agent-native creative systems have not peaked; their next test is interoperability among structured scenes, rendering engines, and review workflows. Native recompilation also merits attention if the cluster produces reusable tooling rather than isolated game ports.
